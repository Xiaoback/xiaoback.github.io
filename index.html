Pension Portability, Labor Mobility, and Regional Convergence: Evidence from China

with Ming Lu and Huiyong Zhong

Journal of Asian Economics, 104 (2026), 102158.

Abstract

Fragmented pension systems hinder labor mobility and perpetuate regional inequality. This paper evaluates China’s provincial pension pooling reform designed to enhance pension portability within provinces. Using a staggered difference-in-differences approach on individual and provincial panel data from 2014-2020, we find the reform increased intra-provincial migration, particularly for low-skilled workers. Using the reform as an instrumental variable, we show migration improved job matching, evidenced by higher income and job satisfaction. The reform also contributed to higher provincial labor productivity and reduced within-province inequality. Our findings demonstrate that labor mobility promotes regional convergence through the lens of improving pension portability.

A Win-Win for Population Quality and Allocation Dividends: A Spatial Political Economy Perspective

with Ming Lu

Comparative Economic & Social Systems (in Chinese), No. 1, 2025.

Abstract

China’s economy currently faces significant challenges, including an aging population and declining birth rates. To address these issues, it is imperative to adopt a regional development strategy that aligns with the overarching, long-term goals of enhancing population quality and optimizing allocation dividends. From the perspective of spatial political economy, this article identifies several key obstacles to achieving these goals. These include the short-term, localized development priorities of local governments, the spatial externalities arising from population mobility, and the limitations imposed on access to public services for mobile populations by the household registration system. Addressing these challenges requires a shift in mindset and a deepening of reform efforts. It is essential to acknowledge the role of regional agglomeration in balancing education supply-demand disparities and stimulating demand for education in areas experiencing population inflows and outflows. Furthermore, the central government must take on greater responsibility in coordinating local governments to mitigate spatial externalities and establish a public education service mechanism responsive to population dynamics. By doing so, a win-win outcome for both population quality and allocation dividends can be achieved.
