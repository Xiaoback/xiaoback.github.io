Pension Portability, Labor Mobility, and Regional Convergence: Evidence from China

with Ming Lu and Huiyong Zhong

Journal of Asian Economics, 104 (2026), 102158.

Abstract

This paper evaluates China's provincial pension pooling reform, which aimed to improve pension portability within provinces. Using a staggered difference-in-differences approach with individual and provincial panel data from 2014–2020, it finds that the reform increased intra-provincial migration, particularly among low-skilled workers. Instrumental-variable estimates indicate that induced migration improved job matching, as reflected in higher income and job satisfaction. At the provincial level, the reform contributed to higher labor productivity and reduced within-province inequality.

A Win-Win for Population Quality and Allocation Dividends: A Spatial Political Economy Perspective

with Ming Lu

Comparative Economic & Social Systems (in Chinese), No. 1, 2025.

Abstract

Against the backdrop of population aging and declining fertility, this article examines how China can achieve both population-quality and allocation dividends. From a spatial political economy perspective, it argues that local governments' short-term, place-based incentives, the spatial externalities generated by population mobility, and hukou-based restrictions on migrants' access to public services impede these goals. It highlights the need for stronger central coordination to address spatial externalities and for a basic public education service system that adapts to demographic change.
